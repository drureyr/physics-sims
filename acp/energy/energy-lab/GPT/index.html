Teacher notes & model assumptions

The ball in the bowl slides without rotating; coaster is a point mass constrained to the track. Track resistance is a constant opposing force F = k·mg, not a general model of wheel friction. Heat denotes accumulated dissipated energy in the object + surroundings. Track is sampled into short straight segments. Earth uses g = 10; Moon uses g = 1.6. The drop ends just before ground impact. Bounce losses occur at impacts only; the final tiny motion is truncated.

History and connections

Willem 's Gravesande (1688–1742), circa 1722: dropping balls into clay helped establish the importance of speed squared. Émilie du Châtelet (1706–1749) discussed the vis viva evidence in 1740.

Count Rumford (1753–1814), 1798: cannon-boring experiments connected mechanical energy and heating. Rumford was born in Woburn, Massachusetts.

James Prescott Joule (1818–1889), 1840s: falling weights drove paddles in water, demonstrating a quantitative connection between mechanical energy and thermal energy.

Connections: hydroelectric dams, roller coaster design, and energy dissipation in collisions.
