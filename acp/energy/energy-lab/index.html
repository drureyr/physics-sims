How we learned that PE + KE stays the same

The ideas in this simulation took about 170 years to sort out, from the 1680s to the 1850s.

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)

A German mathematician and philosopher. In 1686 he argued that a moving body carries a "living force" (vis viva) equal to mv², and that this quantity is what nature keeps constant. That is the ancestor of kinetic energy.

Willem 's Gravesande (1688–1742) and Émilie du Châtelet (1706–1749)

Around 1722, the Dutch scientist 's Gravesande dropped brass balls into soft clay. A ball dropped from 4 times the height hit with only twice the speed, but it sank 4 times as deep. In 1740 the French scientist du Châtelet used his results in her physics textbook to show that the energy of motion depends on v², not v.

Thomas Young (1773–1829) and Gaspard-Gustave Coriolis (1792–1843)

The English scientist Young first used the word energy in its modern sense in 1807. In 1829 the French engineer Coriolis added the ½, giving KE = ½mv², and gave the word work its physics meaning.

William Rankine (1820–1872)

A Scottish engineer who coined the term potential energy in 1853. By then Mayer, Joule and Helmholtz had shown in the 1840s that energy is never created or destroyed.

Count Rumford (1753–1814) and James Prescott Joule (1818–1889)

Where does energy go when friction slows things down? In 1798, Rumford (born Benjamin Thompson in Massachusetts) noticed that boring out cannon barrels in Munich made endless heat from friction alone. In the 1840s, Joule used a falling weight to spin a paddle wheel in water and measured how much the water warmed up. Mechanical energy that seems to disappear shows up as heat.

Connections

History and engineering: America's first roller coaster, LaMarcus Thompson's Switchback Railway, opened at Coney Island in 1884. Riders were pushed to the top of a tower, then coasted down on gravity alone. Designers still follow the rule this simulation shows: the first hill must be the tallest.

Geology: Water held high behind a dam has PE. Hoover Dam (finished 1936) lets water fall about 180 m, turning PE into KE that spins the generators.

Biology: A kangaroo stores energy in its tendons on each landing and gets much of it back on the next hop, trading energy back and forth like the ball in the bowl.
